GUITAR EXERCISES - SECTION VI, LEVEL 49
CHORD MECHANICS, MUTING, AND OPEN-CHORD FOUNDATION

PURPOSE
Build eight essential open chords and three open power chords with minimal fretting pressure, accurate string exclusion, clean release, and economical changes at whole-, half-, and quarter-note speed.

CORE CHORDS
Em E Am A Dm D C G
POWER CHORDS
E5 A5 D5

COMPLETE NOTATED WORKLOAD: 780 pick or strum attacks before optional repeats.


========================================================================================================================
CHORD-DIAGRAM LEGEND
========================================================================================================================
Strings are printed low E to high e. X = mute. O = open. Numbers 1-4 are fretting-hand fingers.
Pink/root markers in the PDF identify the chord root. Blue markers identify the other chord tones.
Place fingertips just behind the fret wire. Keep the thumb and wrist relaxed. Begin every strum on the printed bass string.

========================================================================================================================
MODULE A1 - MINIMUM FRETTING PRESSURE
========================================================================================================================
index finger: G1 (G#) - four attacks: clean; reduce to buzz threshold; restore minimum clean pressure; release without lifting.
middle finger: A2 (B) - four attacks: clean; reduce to buzz threshold; restore minimum clean pressure; release without lifting.
ring finger: D2 (E) - four attacks: clean; reduce to buzz threshold; restore minimum clean pressure; release without lifting.
index finger: B1 (C) - four attacks: clean; reduce to buzz threshold; restore minimum clean pressure; release without lifting.
middle finger: e2 (F#) - four attacks: clean; reduce to buzz threshold; restore minimum clean pressure; release without lifting.
ring finger: B3 (D) - four attacks: clean; reduce to buzz threshold; restore minimum clean pressure; release without lifting.
Stop if the hand squeezes, the wrist bends sharply, or a fingertip collapses into a neighboring string.

MODULE J - FOUR-PASS CAPSTONE
========================================================================================================================
Progression: Am | C | G | Em
Pass 1 - sustained quarters
Four downstrokes per bar; allow every chord to ring.
Grid: D D D D
16 attacks

Pass 2 - release after each attack
Strum, release pressure halfway through the beat, reapply before the next attack.
Grid: D . D . D . D .
16 attacks

Pass 3 - chord plus muted backbeat
Pitched chord on beats 1 and 3; muted strum on beats 2 and 4.
Grid: D x D x
16 attacks

Pass 4 - four-string arpeggio
Pick four active chord strings in a stable repeating order.
Grid: 1 2 3 4
16 attacks


========================================================================================================================
PRACTICE PROGRESSION AND ERROR REPAIR
========================================================================================================================
1. 1. Diagram legend and posture
2. 2. Pressure calibration
3. 3. Muting audit
4. 4. E/Em and A/Am
5. 5. D/Dm and C/G
6. 6. Power chords
7. 7. Whole-note changes
8. 8. Half-note changes
9. 9. Quarter-note changes
10. 10. Progression capstone
BUZZ: move closer to the fret before adding pressure.
DEAD OPEN STRING: increase finger curvature.
WRONG BASS: aim the first stroke at the printed string.
X STRING RINGS: add a relaxed muting touch or narrow the strum.
LATE CHORD: prepare during the prior sustain.
FINGERS FLY: freeze and move one finger at a time.
EARLY RELEASE: hold through the full count.
PAIN OR TENSION: stop, reduce duration, and use less pressure.

- Complete whole-, half-, and quarter-note grids on time.
- Demonstrate all eight common/pivot/guide transitions.
- Play three major and three minor progressions from memory.
- Complete the Am-C-G-Em capstone on two separate days.

NEXT LEVEL
Level 50 - chord transitions and common-finger movement. The same shapes will move into smaller rhythmic windows, anticipated changes, release timing, and longer progression studies.
