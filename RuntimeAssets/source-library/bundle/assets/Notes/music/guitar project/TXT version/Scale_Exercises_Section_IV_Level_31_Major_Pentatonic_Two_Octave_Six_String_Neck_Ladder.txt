SCALE EXERCISES - SECTION IV, LEVEL 31
MAJOR PENTATONIC TWO-OCTAVE SIX-STRING NECK LADDER: A TO A

PURPOSE
Extend the exact Level 30 major-pentatonic frame across all six strings and two full octaves. Play the complete
22-note up/down cell, release pressure, move the entire hand-and-thumb frame exactly one fret, and repeat through
every major-pentatonic key. Begin with A major pentatonic at low-E fret 5, climb to A major pentatonic at fret 17,
then reverse and return one fret at a time.

IMPORTANT INTERPRETATION
Moving one fixed shape by one fret transposes it. A major pentatonic is the starting rung, not the key of every rung.
Holding A major pentatonic constant while connecting its five positions is a later branch of Section IV.

MAJOR-PENTATONIC FORMULA
Degrees per octave: 1 2 3 5 6
Intervals: whole - whole - minor third - whole - minor third
The omitted degrees 4 and 7 remain absent in both octaves.

COMPACT TWO-OCTAVE SIX-STRING SHAPE - ROOT FRET R ON LOW E
Low E string: R, R+2               Fingers 2,4
A string:     R-1, R+2             Fingers 1,4
D string:     R-1, R+2             Fingers 1,4
G string:     R-1, R+1             Fingers 1,3
B string:     R, R+2               Fingers 2,4
High e string:R                    Finger 2
The four-fret hand frame runs from R-1 through R+2.

STRING-DISTRIBUTION RULE
Ascending note counts by string: 2 - 2 - 2 - 2 - 2 - 1.
Descending note counts by string: 1 - 2 - 2 - 2 - 2 - 2.
The G-to-B tuning offset changes the visible geometry. Rebuild the assigned fingering rather than copying the
G-string shape directly onto the B and high-e strings.

CELL AND PICKING RULE
Ascend from the low root through the middle root to the high root, repeat the high root at the turnaround,
then descend to the low root. The complete cell contains 22 notes, so the next key begins with the same
alternate-picking orientation.
D-start: strict D-U alternate picking through every crossing, root, turnaround, and one-fret relocation.
U-start: strict U-D alternate picking through every crossing, root, turnaround, and one-fret relocation.
Do not restart the pick cycle on a new string, at the middle root, at the high-root turn, or after shifting.

NECK ROUTE
UP:   A(5) > Bb(6) > B(7) > C(8) > Db(9) > D(10) > Eb(11) > E(12) > F(13) > Gb(14) > G(15) > Ab(16) > A(17)
DOWN: A(17) > Ab(16) > G(15) > Gb(14) > F(13) > E(12) > Eb(11) > D(10) > Db(9) > C(8) > B(7) > Bb(6) > A(5)

PRACTICE RULE
Begin 10-15 BPM below your clean Level 30 tempo. First master the A-major-pentatonic cell alone. Then use 2-3
neighboring rungs, play even eighth notes, and pause one beat after each 22-note cell while moving the complete
frame one fret. Repeat with both pick starts. Remove the pause, connect the ladder, reverse at fret 17, and return
to fret 5. Add 2 BPM after three clean round trips. Reduce 4-6 BPM after two errors, crossing noise, dragged shifts,
or rising tension. Advance after the full route is clean with both pick starts on two separate days.

FULL ROUND-TRIP CHALLENGE
1. Start with A major pentatonic at low-E fret 5.
2. Play all 13 cells without restarting the alternate-picking cycle.
3. Reverse after the fret-17 cell and return through rungs 12-01.
4. Name each key and sing or say 1-2-3-5-6 through both octaves.
5. Repeat the route with the opposite starting stroke.
One complete route contains 25 cells x 22 notes = 550 picked notes.

CLEAN-ROUND STANDARD
No open strings, missing pentatonic tone, unplanned doubled note, dragged shift, crossing hesitation, or volume
drop at the middle or high root. The one-fret relocations must not change the tempo.

AFTER THE LADDER IS CLEAN
Root accents: accent only the low, middle, and high roots.
Chord-tone focus: accent degrees 1, 3, and 5; hear degrees 2 and 6 as color tones.
String-entry audit: accent the first note on every new string, then remove all landing accents.
Direction: begin selected sessions at fret 17 and descend first.
Random entry: choose any root fret from 5-17, name the key, and continue either way.

MASTERY GATE
Complete the full route with both pick starts on two separate days. Name every key, preserve the five-note formula
in both octaves, keep equal note length and volume, and recover the correct key after a deliberate random stop.
