SCALE EXERCISES - SECTION V, LEVEL 43
A BLUES SCALE MAP AND BLUE-NOTE CONTROL - FIXED KEY A

PURPOSE
Add Eb, the b5 blue note, to the complete A-minor-pentatonic map from Levels 41-42. Learn every
practical Eb location, insert it into all five positions, and control whether it passes quickly, resolves
to degree 4 or 5, slides chromatically, or remains exposed as deliberate tension.

A BLUES FORMULA
Notes:    A C D Eb E G
Degrees:  1 b3 4 b5 5 b7
Intervals: minor third - whole - half - half - minor third - whole
A minor pentatonic remains the five-note skeleton. Eb is the added b5; it is not a replacement for D or E.

BLUE-NOTE PRINCIPLE
The b5 is a controlled tension, not an automatically forbidden note and not an automatically stable one.
It may pass between 4 and 5, slide chromatically, or be sustained deliberately. The exercise fails only
when the duration or resolution is accidental.

NOTATION
h = hammer-on | / = slide upward | \ = slide downward | ~~~~ = sustain or vibrato | R = measured rest

========================================================================================================================
PRACTICE PROGRESSION
========================================================================================================================
1. Name all six core Eb locations and the A-string fret-18 extension.
2. Play the one-octave cell at one note per click with both pick starts.
3. Link the two-octave six-string cell without resetting at either octave.
4. Compare the pentatonic skeleton and blues insertion in one position at a time.
5. Complete all six 4-b5-5 and 5-b5-4 triplet cells.
6. Add the five four-note resolution pairs as straight sixteenths.
7. Contrast passing Eb with sustained Eb on all six strings.
8. Convert the picked chromatic cells into two-attack slide chains.
9. Link the four position seams and complete all three displacement studies.
10. Play the six audit cards and change the role of Eb on command.

ERROR DIAGNOSIS
MISSING b5: say 4-b5-5 before playing and isolate one three-fret chromatic site.
WRONG SPELLING: in A blues the added pitch is Eb, not D#.
RUSHED SEMITONES: return to one note per click; make D-Eb and Eb-E equal.
ACCIDENTAL RESTING TONE: decide before the phrase whether Eb passes or sustains.
EXTRA SLIDE ATTACK: mute the string and rehearse two attacks for six sounding tones.
RIGID STRETCH: release and reform for the one-fret extension; do not hold every fret at once.
PICK RESET: air-pick the complete position or reference-cell count.
TENSION OR PAIN: stop, lower the tempo, and reduce the position span.

ADVANCEMENT RULES
Add 2 BPM after three clean runs at the current module length. After two errors, reduce 4-6 BPM or shorten
the exercise to one chromatic site. Increase route length before speed when the map is weak. Increase speed
only after the intended role of Eb remains audible.
