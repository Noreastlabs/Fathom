SCALE EXERCISES - SECTION IV, LEVEL 30
MAJOR PENTATONIC ONE-OCTAVE ONE-FRET NECK LADDER: A TO A

PURPOSE
Learn one compact one-octave major-pentatonic shape, move the complete hand-and-thumb frame exactly one fret,
and repeat through every major-pentatonic key. Begin with A major pentatonic at low-E fret 5, climb to A major
pentatonic at fret 17, then reverse and return one fret at a time.

MAJOR SCALE VS MAJOR PENTATONIC
A major:             A B C# D E F# G# A
A major pentatonic:  A B C#   E F#    A
Degrees 4 and 7 are omitted. This removes the two half-step tensions 3-4 and 7-8 from the full major scale.

MAJOR-PENTATONIC FORMULA
Degrees:   1 2 3 5 6 8
Intervals: whole - whole - minor third - whole - minor third

COMPACT ONE-OCTAVE SHAPE - ROOT FRET R ON LOW E
Low E string: R, R+2               Fingers 2,4
A string:     R-1, R+2             Fingers 1,4
D string:     R-1, R+2             Fingers 1,4
The four-fret hand frame runs from R-1 through R+2.
Ascending notes per string: 2-2-2. Descending notes per string: 2-2-2.

CELL AND PICKING RULE
Play degrees 1-2-3-5-6-8, then 8-6-5-3-2-1. The octave root is repeated at the turn.
This creates 12 notes, so the next key begins with the same alternate-picking orientation.
D-start: D U D U D U | D U D U D U
U-start: U D U D U D | U D U D U D
Do not restart the pick cycle at the scale turn, a string crossing, or the one-fret shift.

IMPORTANT INTERPRETATION
Moving one fixed shape by one fret transposes it. A major pentatonic is the starting rung, not the key of every rung.
Holding A major pentatonic constant while connecting its five neck positions is a later branch of this section.

NECK ROUTE
UP:   A(5) > Bb(6) > B(7) > C(8) > Db(9) > D(10) > Eb(11) > E(12) > F(13) > Gb(14) > G(15) > Ab(16) > A(17)
DOWN: A(17) > Ab(16) > G(15) > Gb(14) > F(13) > E(12) > Eb(11) > D(10) > Db(9) > C(8) > B(7) > Bb(6) > A(5)

PRACTICE RULE
First compare A major and A major pentatonic and identify the omitted 4th and 7th. Then use 3-4 neighboring
major-pentatonic rungs. Play even eighth notes and pause one beat after each 12-note cell. During the pause,
release pressure and move the complete frame one fret. Repeat with both pick starts. Then remove the pause,
connect the ladder, reverse at fret 17, and return to fret 5.
Add 2 BPM after three clean round trips. Reduce 4-6 BPM after two errors, dragged shift noise, or rising tension.
Advance after the complete route is clean with both pick starts on two separate days.

FULL ROUND-TRIP CHALLENGE
1. Start with A major pentatonic at fret 5.
2. Play all 13 cells without restarting the alternate-picking cycle.
3. Reverse at fret 17 and return through rungs 12-01.
4. Name each key and say 1-2-3-5-6 before its first note.
5. Repeat with the opposite starting stroke.
One complete route contains 25 cells x 12 notes = 300 picked notes.

CLEAN-ROUND STANDARD
No open strings, no dragged shift noise, no missing scale tone, no hesitation at the balanced 2-2-2 crossings,
and no tempo change at the one-fret relocations.

AFTER THE BASIC LADDER IS CLEAN
Full-scale contrast: play one A-major cell, then one A-major-pentatonic cell; hear and locate the omitted 4 and 7.
Chord-tone focus: accent degrees 1, 3, and 5; then hear degrees 2 and 6 as color tones.
Rhythm: eighth notes, straight sixteenths, then six-note ascent and descent bursts.
Direction: begin some sessions at fret 17 and descend first.
Random entry: choose any root fret from 5-17, name the major key, play its cell, then continue either way.

MASTERY GATE
Complete the full route with both pick starts on two separate days. Name every key, preserve the 1-2-3-5-6 formula,
keep equal note length and volume, and recover the correct key after a deliberate random stop.
