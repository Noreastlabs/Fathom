SCALE EXERCISES - SECTION II, LEVEL 13
MAJOR SCALE TWO-OCTAVE SIX-STRING NECK LADDER: A TO A

PURPOSE
Extend the compact Level 12 major-scale frame across all six strings and two full octaves. Play the
complete 30-note up/down cell, release pressure, move the entire hand-and-thumb frame exactly one fret,
and repeat through every major key. Begin with A major at low-E fret 5, climb to A major at fret 17,
then reverse and return one fret at a time.

IMPORTANT INTERPRETATION
Moving one fixed scale shape by one fret transposes it. A major is the starting rung, not the key of every rung.
A fixed-key A-major exercise in every neck position requires changing the note layout and remains a separate branch.

MAJOR-SCALE FORMULA
Degrees: 1 2 3 4 5 6 7, repeated in the second octave
Intervals: whole - whole - half - whole - whole - whole - half

COMPACT TWO-OCTAVE SHAPE - ROOT FRET R ON LOW E
Low E string: R, R+2               Fingers 2,4
A string:     R-1, R, R+2          Fingers 1,2,4
D string:     R-1, R+1, R+2        Fingers 1,3,4
G string:     R-1, R+1, R+2        Fingers 1,3,4
B string:     R, R+2               Fingers 2,4
High e string:R-1, R               Fingers 1,2
The four-fret hand frame still runs from R-1 through R+2.

STRING-DISTRIBUTION RULE
Ascending note counts by string: 2 - 3 - 3 - 3 - 2 - 2.
Descending note counts by string: 2 - 2 - 3 - 3 - 3 - 2.
The B-string tuning offset changes the visible pattern. Rebuild the assigned fingering instead of copying
the G-string geometry onto the B and high-e strings.

CELL AND PICKING RULE
Ascend from the low root through the middle octave to the high root, repeat the high root at the turn,
then descend to the low root. The result is 30 notes, so the next key begins with the same pick orientation.
D-start: strict D-U alternate picking through every string crossing, the turn, and the one-fret relocation.
U-start: strict U-D alternate picking through every string crossing, the turn, and the one-fret relocation.
Do not restart the pick cycle on a new string, at either octave, at the scale turn, or after shifting one fret.

NECK ROUTE
UP:   A(5) > Bb(6) > B(7) > C(8) > Db(9) > D(10) > Eb(11) > E(12) > F(13) > Gb(14) > G(15) > Ab(16) > A(17)
DOWN: A(17) > Ab(16) > G(15) > Gb(14) > F(13) > E(12) > Eb(11) > D(10) > Db(9) > C(8) > B(7) > Bb(6) > A(5)

PRACTICE RULE
Begin 10-15 BPM below your clean Level 12 tempo. First master the A-major cell alone. Then use 2-3 neighboring
rungs, play even eighth notes, and pause one beat after each 30-note cell while moving the complete frame one fret.
Repeat with both pick starts. Remove the pause, connect the ladder, reverse at fret 17, and return to fret 5.
Add 2 BPM after three clean round trips. Reduce 4-6 BPM after two errors, string-crossing noise, dragged shifts,
or rising tension. Advance after the full route is clean with both pick starts on two separate days.

FULL ROUND-TRIP CHALLENGE
Start at A major, low-E fret 5. Play rungs 01-13 without restarting the pick cycle. Reverse after the
fret-17 A-major cell and return through rungs 12-01. Say each key name aloud before its low root.
Repeat the complete route with the opposite starting stroke.

CLEAN-ROUND STANDARD
No open strings; no wrong accidental; no missing or doubled note except the intentional repeated high root;
no scrape or tempo change at the one-fret relocations; no hesitation at the D-to-G, G-to-B, or B-to-e crossings;
and equal volume between the lower and upper octaves.

AFTER THE BASIC LADDER IS CLEAN
1. Octave accents: accent only the low, middle, and high roots while keeping every other note even.
2. String-entry accents: accent the first note on each new string, then remove those accents.
3. Direction: begin selected sessions at fret 17 and descend first.
4. Random entry: choose any root fret from 5-17, name the key, play its cell, and continue in either direction.
5. Rhythm: eighth notes, straight sixteenths, then 6-note bursts separated by one beat of rest.
These are Level 13 variations, not separate full levels.
