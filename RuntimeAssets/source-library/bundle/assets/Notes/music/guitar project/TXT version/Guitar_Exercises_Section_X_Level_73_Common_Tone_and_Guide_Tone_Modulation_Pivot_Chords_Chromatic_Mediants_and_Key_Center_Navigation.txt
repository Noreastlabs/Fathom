GUITAR EXERCISES - SECTION X, LEVEL 73
COMMON-TONE AND GUIDE-TONE MODULATION, PIVOT CHORDS, CHROMATIC MEDIANTS, AND KEY-CENTER NAVIGATION

PURPOSE
Move between tonal centers while preserving phrase identity, voice leading, rhythm, and fretboard orientation. Related-key modulations use pivot chords; distant chromatic-mediant changes retain one or more common tones and are confirmed by the destination dominant or tonic.

THREE MODULATION MECHANISMS
Pivot chord: one chord receives a Roman-numeral function in both keys.
Common-tone modulation: one pitch remains while the surrounding harmony changes.
Guide-tone modulation: inner voices move by semitone or remain common while the bass and tonic center change.

CORE EXAMPLES
Cmaj7-Am7-D7-Gmaj7: Am7 is vi in C and ii in G.
Cmaj7-Em7-A7-Dmaj7: Em7 is iii in C and ii in D.
Cmaj7-Dm7-C7-Fmaj7: Dm7 is ii in C and vi in F.
Cmaj7 -> Emaj7: common tones E and B.
Cmaj7 -> Abmaj7: common tones C and G.

ERROR DIAGNOSIS
KEY CHANGE TOO WEAK: lengthen the destination dominant and tonic.
PIVOT SOUNDS RANDOM: state both Roman-numeral functions before playing.
COMMON TONE LOST: sustain or repeat it through the barline.
MELODY SOUNDS RESTARTED: preserve motif rhythm and contour.
VOICE LEAP TOO LARGE: choose another octave or inversion.
SOURCE KEY RETURNS: avoid source-only tones after destination V-I.
