SCALE EXERCISES - SECTION III, LEVEL 22
NATURAL MINOR TWO-OCTAVE SIX-STRING NECK LADDER: A TO A

PURPOSE
Extend the Level 21 natural-minor shape across all six strings and two full octaves. Play the
complete 30-note up/down cell, release pressure, move the entire hand-and-thumb frame exactly one fret,
and repeat through every natural-minor key. Begin with A natural minor at low-E fret 5, climb to A
natural minor at fret 17, then reverse and return one fret at a time.

IMPORTANT INTERPRETATION
Moving one fixed scale shape by one fret transposes it. A natural minor is the starting rung, not the key
of every rung. A fixed-key A-minor exercise in every neck position requires changing the note layout and
remains a separate later branch.

NATURAL-MINOR FORMULA
Degrees:   1 2 b3 4 5 b6 b7, repeated in the second octave
Intervals: whole - half - whole - whole - half - whole - whole

TWO-OCTAVE SHAPE - ROOT FRET R ON LOW E
Low E string: R, R+2, R+3          Fingers 1,3,4
A string:     R, R+2, R+3          Fingers 1,3,4
D string:     R, R+2               Fingers 1,3
G string:     R-1, R, R+2          Fingers 1,2,4
B string:     R, R+1, R+3          Fingers 1,2,4
High e string:R                     Finger 1
The complete map spans R-1 through R+3. Do not hold a static five-fret stretch. Keep the lower-string
frame relaxed, release pressure for the one-fret G-string backreach, and rebuild the hand for the B string.

STRING-DISTRIBUTION RULE
Ascending note counts by string: 3 - 3 - 2 - 3 - 3 - 1.
Descending note counts by string: 1 - 3 - 3 - 2 - 3 - 3.
The G string begins one fret below the root reference, and the B-string tuning offset changes the visible
pattern again. Use the assigned fingering rather than copying one string pattern onto the next.

CELL AND PICKING RULE
Ascend from the low root through the middle octave to the high root, repeat the high root at the turn,
then descend to the low root. The result is 30 notes, so the next key begins with the same pick orientation.
D-start: strict D-U alternate picking through every string crossing, the turn, and the one-fret relocation.
U-start: strict U-D alternate picking through every string crossing, the turn, and the one-fret relocation.
Do not restart the pick cycle on a new string, at either octave, at the scale turn, or after shifting one fret.

NECK ROUTE
UP:   A(5) > Bb(6) > B(7) > C(8) > C#(9) > D(10) > Eb(11) > E(12) > F(13) > F#(14) > G(15) > G#(16) > A(17)
DOWN: A(17) > G#(16) > G(15) > F#(14) > F(13) > E(12) > Eb(11) > D(10) > C#(9) > C(8) > B(7) > Bb(6) > A(5)

PRACTICE RULE
Begin 10-15 BPM below your clean Level 21 tempo. First master the A-natural-minor cell alone. Then use
2-3 neighboring rungs, play even eighth notes, and pause one beat after each 30-note cell while moving
the complete frame one fret. Repeat with both pick starts. Remove the pause, connect the ladder, reverse
at fret 17, and return to fret 5. Add 2 BPM after three clean round trips. Reduce 4-6 BPM after two errors,
string-crossing noise, dragged backreaches, or rising tension. Advance after the full route is clean with
both pick starts on two separate days.

FULL ROUND-TRIP CHALLENGE
1. Start with A natural minor at fret 5.
2. Play all 13 cells without restarting the alternate-picking cycle.
3. Reverse after the fret-17 A-natural-minor cell and return through rungs 12-01.
4. Name each key and say the root before its first note.
5. Repeat with the opposite starting stroke.
One complete route contains 25 cells x 30 notes = 750 picked notes.

CLEAN-ROUND STANDARD
No open strings; no wrong accidental; no missing or doubled note except the intentional repeated high root;
no dragged G-string backreach; no scrape or tempo change at the one-fret relocations; no hesitation at the
D-to-G, G-to-B, or B-to-e crossings; and equal volume between the lower and upper octaves.

AFTER THE BASIC LADDER IS CLEAN
1. Octave accents: accent only the low, middle, and high roots while keeping every other note even.
2. Backreach audit: isolate D-to-G; release pressure before the G-string index reaches one fret lower.
3. String-entry accents: accent the first note on each new string, then remove those accents.
4. Crossing audit: isolate G-to-B and B-to-e transitions; keep the landing note unaccented.
5. Direction: begin selected sessions at fret 17 and descend first.
6. Random entry: choose any root fret from 5-17, name the key, play its cell, and continue either direction.
These are Level 22 variations, not separate full levels.
