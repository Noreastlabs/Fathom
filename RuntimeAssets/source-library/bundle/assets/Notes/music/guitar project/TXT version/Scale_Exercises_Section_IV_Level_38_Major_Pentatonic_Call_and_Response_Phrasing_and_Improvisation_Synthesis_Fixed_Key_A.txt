SCALE EXERCISES - SECTION IV, LEVEL 38
A MAJOR PENTATONIC CALL-AND-RESPONSE PHRASING AND IMPROVISATION SYNTHESIS
FIXED KEY A - FIVE POSITIONS - HARMONY A, D, AND E MAJOR

PURPOSE
Combine the five fixed-key positions, horizontal movement, expressive articulation, and I-IV-V chord targets
into phrases that sound intentional rather than like scale recitation. Build a short call, leave measured space,
answer the active harmony, develop one recognizable motif, and preserve the chord change while moving across the neck.

A MAJOR PENTATONIC
Notes: A B C# E F#
Degrees: 1 2 3 5 6
I-IV-V chords: A major = A C# E; D major = D F# A; E major = E G# B.
Strong pentatonic targets: A/C#/E over A; F#/A over D; E/B over E.
Completion tones: D* may appear only over D major; G#* may appear only over E major.

PHRASE GRAMMAR
A call states a rhythm and contour, then leaves room. It often ends on degree 2 or 6 rather than a final tonic.
A response may echo the rhythm, reverse the contour, move to a neighboring position, or change the chord target.
Silence is counted. A rest is not lost time; it is part of the phrase. A response should arrive because the harmony
changed, not because the player ran out of scale notes.

TAB LEGEND
P = picked attack; h = hammer-on destination; p = pull-off destination; s = slide destination;
b = bend destination; r = rest; = = held tone. Continue alternate picking only through actual picked attacks.
The count row shows eighth-note slots: 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &.

CORE WRITTEN COUNTS
Module A local question/answer pairs: 55 sounding tones / 37 pick attacks.
Module B four seam phrases: 97 sounding tones / 62 pick attacks.
Module C motif transformation lab: 30 sounding tones / 23 pick attacks.
Module D space and density ladder: 60 sounding tones / 60 pick attacks.
Module E model 12-bar chorus: 70 sounding tones / 55 pick attacks.
Complete written workout: 312 sounding tones / 237 pick attacks, before the controlled free choruses.

MODULE F - THREE-CHORUS CONTROLLED IMPROVISATION SYNTHESIS
========================================================================================================================
CHORUS 1 - SKELETON AND SPACE
Use P1 and P2 only. Maximum 3 sounding tones per bar. Land one strong target on every chord change and leave at
least one complete beat of silence in every two-bar pair. No D* or G#*.

CHORUS 2 - MOTIF DEVELOPMENT AND POSITION MOVEMENT
Choose one seed motif from Module C. Use one response transformation per two-bar pair. Move through at least four
positions, cross at least two seams, and use no more than one articulation in each two-bar phrase.

CHORUS 3 - FREE SYNTHESIS
Use all five positions and horizontal lanes. Preserve strong beat-1 targets, include at least two bars of deliberate
space, use D* and G#* only on their supporting chords, and finish on an A root without a last-second fret search.

RESPONSE CARDS
1 Echo the call rhythm with a new target. 2 Reverse the contour. 3 Move the answer to the next position.
4 Delay the answer by an eighth note. 5 Shorten the answer and leave beat 4 empty. 6 Expand the answer with one
legato, slide, bend, or vibrato event while preserving the target time.

PRACTICE PROGRESSION
1. Clap and speak the call/response rhythm before touching the guitar.
2. Complete all five local pairs; exaggerate the silence between sentences.
3. Play the four seam phrases and verify every beat-1 target.
4. Perform the seed plus all six transformation options without changing tempo.
5. Play sparse, medium, and dense versions at one unchanged BPM.
6. Learn the 12-bar model chorus in four-bar blocks, then link the full route.
7. Replace only response bars while preserving the written calls.
8. Record the three-chorus capstone and score it before increasing tempo.

ERROR DIAGNOSIS
CALL AND RESPONSE SOUND IDENTICAL: change one element only - target, contour, position, density, or articulation.
RESPONSE ARRIVES LATE: sing the target before the barline and shorten the call by one note.
NO SPACE: remove beat 4 from every call and one additional beat from every response.
MOTIF DISAPPEARS: keep the rhythm fixed and change only the ending target.
POSITION SHIFT SOUNDS LIKE A BREAK: isolate the final call note, seam movement, and first response target.
BEND OR SLIDE HIDES THE PULSE: remove the articulation; restore subdivision; add it back after timing is clean.
D* OR G#* LEAKS INTO THE WRONG CHORD: return to the five-note pool and rehearse the 12-bar target schedule aloud.
TENSION OR PANIC: stop the free chorus, return to one four-bar phrase, and reduce 4-6 BPM.

ADVANCEMENT RULES
Harmony, phrase identity, timing, and pitch control outrank speed. Add 2 BPM after three clean choruses at the
current constraint. After two missed targets or lost phrase boundaries, shorten to four bars or reduce 4-6 BPM.
Use clean tone until the rests, landings, slides, bends, and muting are reliable.

MASTERY GATE - SECTION IV COMPLETE
Play all five local pairs and all four seam phrases; transform one motif in six ways; perform sparse, medium, and
dense versions without moving the targets; play the 12-bar model chorus from memory; record the three-chorus
capstone on two separate days; make every chord change audible without accompaniment; use at least one full beat
of deliberate silence in every two-bar pair; use D* and G#* only on IV and V; and end on an A root by choice.
