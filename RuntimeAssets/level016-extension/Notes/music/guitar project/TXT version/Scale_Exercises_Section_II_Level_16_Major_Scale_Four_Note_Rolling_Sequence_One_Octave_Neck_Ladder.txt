SCALE EXERCISES - SECTION II, LEVEL 16
MAJOR SCALE FOUR-NOTE ROLLING SEQUENCE - ONE-OCTAVE NECK LADDER: A TO A

PURPOSE
Turn the compact one-octave major scale into overlapping four-note groups. Complete the full
40-note up/down rolling cell, release pressure, move the entire hand-and-thumb frame exactly
one fret, and repeat through every major key. Begin with A major at low-E fret 5, climb to
A major at fret 17, then reverse and return one fret at a time.

WHY THIS LEVEL RETURNS TO ONE OCTAVE
Level 15 expanded the three-note sequence across two octaves and all six strings. Level 16
deliberately returns to the compact three-string octave so the new four-note overlap can be
learned without simultaneously managing the upper-octave B-string offset. Level 17 can extend
this exact sequence across the complete two-octave frame.

IMPORTANT INTERPRETATION
Moving one fixed scale shape by one fret transposes it. A major is the starting rung, not the
key of every rung. A fixed-key A-major exercise in every neck position requires changing the
note layout and remains a separate branch.

MAJOR-SCALE FORMULA
Degrees: 1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8
Intervals: whole - whole - half - whole - whole - whole - half

COMPACT ONE-OCTAVE SHAPE - ROOT FRET R ON LOW E
Low E string: R, R+2               Fingers 2,4
A string:     R-1, R, R+2          Fingers 1,2,4
D string:     R-1, R+1, R+2        Fingers 1,3,4
The four-fret hand frame runs from R-1 through R+2.

ROLLING FOUR-NOTE SEQUENCE
UP:   1-2-3-4 | 2-3-4-5 | 3-4-5-6 | 4-5-6-7 | 5-6-7-8
DOWN: 8-7-6-5 | 7-6-5-4 | 6-5-4-3 | 5-4-3-2 | 4-3-2-1
Each new group begins one scale degree farther along. At each group boundary, the line drops
a diatonic third and then continues forward. Three notes overlap from one group to the next.
Do not pause, accent, or reset at a group boundary.

CELL AND PICKING RULE
The complete up/down cell contains 10 groups and 40 notes. The octave root is repeated at the
turn: the last note of 5-6-7-8 is followed by the first note of 8-7-6-5. Because 40 is even,
the next key begins with the same pick orientation. Because every group has four notes, every
group also begins with the same stroke.
D-start groups: D-U-D-U | D-U-D-U | D-U-D-U ... through all 10 groups.
U-start groups: U-D-U-D | U-D-U-D | U-D-U-D ... through all 10 groups.
Never restart the pick cycle at a new group, string crossing, scale turn, or one-fret shift.

NECK ROUTE
UP:   A(5) > Bb(6) > B(7) > C(8) > Db(9) > D(10) > Eb(11) > E(12) > F(13) > Gb(14) > G(15) > Ab(16) > A(17)
DOWN: A(17) > Ab(16) > G(15) > Gb(14) > F(13) > E(12) > Eb(11) > D(10) > Db(9) > C(8) > B(7) > Bb(6) > A(5)

PRACTICE RULE
Use sixteenth notes: one four-note group per beat. Begin at roughly 75 percent of your clean
Level 15 metronome setting; for example, if Level 15 is clean at 80 BPM, begin near 60 BPM.
Master the A-major cell alone, then use 2-3 neighboring rungs and pause one beat after each
40-note cell. During the pause, release pressure and move the complete frame one fret. Repeat
with both pick starts. Then remove the pause, connect the ladder, reverse at fret 17, and return
to fret 5. Add 2 BPM after three clean round trips. Reduce 4-6 BPM after two errors, an uneven
group pulse, dragged shift noise, a crossing accent, or rising tension. Advance after the full
route is clean with both pick starts on two separate days.

FULL ROUND-TRIP CHALLENGE
Play rungs 01-13, reverse after the fret-17 A-major cell, and return through rungs 12-01.
Do not replay rung 13 at the turn. The route contains 25 cells x 40 notes = 1,000 picked notes.
Say each key name aloud and repeat the complete trip with the opposite starting stroke.

CLEAN-ROUND STANDARD
No missing group, wrong scale tone, pick reset, open string, shift scrape, crossing accent,
unequal repeated octave roots, broken sixteenth-note pulse, or rising hand tension.

AFTER THE BASIC LADDER IS CLEAN
1. Accent note 1 of every group, then remove all accents.
2. Repeat four passes, moving the accent to notes 2, 3, and 4.
3. Accent only scale degrees 1 and 8 wherever they appear.
4. Begin at fret 17 and descend first.
5. Enter at a random root fret, name the key, and continue in either direction.
These are practice variations, not separate full levels.
