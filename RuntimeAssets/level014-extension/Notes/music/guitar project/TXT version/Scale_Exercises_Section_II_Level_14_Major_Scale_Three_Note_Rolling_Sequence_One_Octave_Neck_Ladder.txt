SCALE EXERCISES - SECTION II, LEVEL 14
MAJOR SCALE THREE-NOTE ROLLING SEQUENCE - ONE-OCTAVE NECK LADDER: A TO A

PURPOSE
Turn the straight one-octave major scale into overlapping three-note groups. Complete the full
36-note up/down rolling cell, release pressure, move the entire hand-and-thumb frame exactly one fret,
and repeat through every major key. Begin with A major at low-E fret 5, climb to A major at fret 17,
then reverse and return one fret at a time.

WHY THIS LEVEL RETURNS TO ONE OCTAVE
Level 13 expanded the straight scale across two octaves and six strings. Level 14 deliberately returns
to the compact three-string octave so the new sequencing motion can be learned without simultaneously
managing the upper-octave B-string offset. The same rolling sequence can be extended later.

IMPORTANT INTERPRETATION
Moving one fixed scale shape by one fret transposes it. A major is the starting rung, not the key of every rung.
A fixed-key A-major exercise in every neck position requires changing the note layout and remains a separate branch.

MAJOR-SCALE FORMULA
Degrees: 1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8
Intervals: whole - whole - half - whole - whole - whole - half

COMPACT ONE-OCTAVE SHAPE - ROOT FRET R ON LOW E
Low E string: R, R+2               Fingers 2,4
A string:     R-1, R, R+2          Fingers 1,2,4
D string:     R-1, R+1, R+2        Fingers 1,3,4
The four-fret hand frame runs from R-1 through R+2.

ROLLING THREE-NOTE SEQUENCE
UP:   1-2-3 | 2-3-4 | 3-4-5 | 4-5-6 | 5-6-7 | 6-7-8
DOWN: 8-7-6 | 7-6-5 | 6-5-4 | 5-4-3 | 4-3-2 | 3-2-1
Each new triplet starts one scale degree farther along. Between groups, the line rolls back one degree:
1-2-3 then 2-3-4; 4-5-6 then 5-6-7. Do not pause or reset at the group boundary.

CELL AND PICKING RULE
The complete up/down cell contains 12 triplets and 36 notes. The octave root is repeated at the turn:
the last note of 6-7-8 is followed by the first note of 8-7-6. Because 36 is even, the next key begins
with the same pick orientation.
D-start groups: D-U-D | U-D-U | D-U-D | U-D-U ...
U-start groups: U-D-U | D-U-D | U-D-U | D-U-D ...
Do not restart the pick cycle at a new triplet, string crossing, scale turn, or one-fret relocation.

NECK ROUTE
UP:   A(5) > Bb(6) > B(7) > C(8) > Db(9) > D(10) > Eb(11) > E(12) > F(13) > Gb(14) > G(15) > Ab(16) > A(17)
DOWN: A(17) > Ab(16) > G(15) > Gb(14) > F(13) > E(12) > Eb(11) > D(10) > Db(9) > C(8) > B(7) > Bb(6) > A(5)

PRACTICE RULE
Use eighth-note triplets: one three-note group per beat. Begin 12-18 BPM below your clean Level 13 tempo.
Master the A-major cell alone, then use 2-3 neighboring rungs and pause one beat after each 36-note cell.
During the pause, release pressure and move the complete frame one fret. Repeat with both pick starts.
Then remove the pause, connect the ladder, reverse at fret 17, and return to fret 5.
Add 2 BPM after three clean round trips. Reduce 4-6 BPM after two errors, broken triplet pulse, dragged shift
noise, or rising tension. Advance after the complete route is clean with both pick starts on two separate days.

FULL ROUND-TRIP CHALLENGE
Start at A major, low-E fret 5. Play rungs 01-13 without restarting the pick cycle. Reverse after the
fret-17 A-major cell and return through rungs 12-01. Say each key name aloud before its first low root.
Repeat the complete route with the opposite starting stroke.

CLEAN-ROUND STANDARD
No missing or added triplet; no pause at a group boundary; no accidental straight-eighth feel; no wrong scale tone;
no open string; no scrape or tempo change at the one-fret relocation; no accent caused by a string crossing;
and equal volume on both repeated octave-root notes at the turn.

AFTER THE BASIC LADDER IS CLEAN
1. Group accents: accent the first note of each triplet, then remove every group accent.
2. Scale-degree accents: accent only degrees 1 and 8 wherever they occur inside the rolling line.
3. Straight subdivision: play the same 36 notes as straight sixteenths without changing the order.
4. Direction: begin selected sessions at fret 17 and descend first.
5. Random entry: choose any root fret from 5-17, name the key, play its cell, and continue in either direction.
These are Level 14 variations, not separate full levels.
