SCALE EXERCISES - SECTION III, LEVEL 23
NATURAL MINOR THREE-NOTE ROLLING SEQUENCE - ONE-OCTAVE NECK LADDER: A TO A

PURPOSE
Turn the straight one-octave natural-minor scale into overlapping three-note groups. Complete the full
36-note up/down rolling cell, release pressure, move the entire hand-and-thumb frame exactly one fret,
and repeat through every natural-minor key. Begin with A natural minor at low-E fret 5, climb to A natural
minor at fret 17, then reverse and return one fret at a time.

WHY THIS LEVEL RETURNS TO ONE OCTAVE
Level 22 expanded the straight natural-minor scale across two octaves and all six strings. Level 23
deliberately returns to the compact three-string octave so the new overlap pattern can be learned without
also managing the upper-octave backreach and B-string offset. Level 24 will extend the sequence later.

IMPORTANT INTERPRETATION
Moving one fixed scale shape by one fret transposes it. A natural minor is the starting rung, not the key of every rung.
A fixed-key A-natural-minor exercise in every neck position requires changing the note layout and remains a separate branch.

NATURAL-MINOR FORMULA
Degrees:   1 2 b3 4 5 b6 b7 8
Intervals: whole - half - whole - whole - half - whole - whole

COMPACT ONE-OCTAVE SHAPE - ROOT FRET R ON LOW E
Low E string: R, R+2, R+3          Fingers 1,3,4
A string:     R, R+2, R+3          Fingers 1,3,4
D string:     R, R+2               Fingers 1,3
The four-fret hand frame runs from R through R+3.
Ascending notes per string: 3-3-2. Descending notes per string: 2-3-3.

ROLLING THREE-NOTE SEQUENCE
UP:   1-2-b3 | 2-b3-4 | b3-4-5 | 4-5-b6 | 5-b6-b7 | b6-b7-8
DOWN: 8-b7-b6 | b7-b6-5 | b6-5-4 | 5-4-b3 | 4-b3-2 | b3-2-1
Each new triplet starts one scale degree farther along. Between groups, the line rolls back one degree.
Do not pause or reset at a triplet boundary, even when the string crossing moves from late to early.

STRING-CROSSING MAP
UP:   EEE | EEA | EAA | AAA | AAD | ADD
DOWN: DDA | DAA | AAA | AAE | AEE | EEE
The all-one-string groups alternate with late and early crossings. Keep the landing note equal in volume.

CELL AND PICKING RULE
The complete up/down cell contains 12 triplets and 36 notes. The octave root is repeated at the turn:
the last note of b6-b7-8 is followed by the first note of 8-b7-b6. Because 36 is even, the next key
begins with the same pick orientation.
D-start groups: D-U-D | U-D-U | D-U-D | U-D-U ...
U-start groups: U-D-U | D-U-D | U-D-U | D-U-D ...
Do not restart the pick cycle at a new triplet, string crossing, scale turn, or one-fret relocation.

NECK ROUTE
UP:   A(5) > Bb(6) > B(7) > C(8) > C#(9) > D(10) > Eb(11) > E(12) > F(13) > F#(14) > G(15) > G#(16) > A(17)
DOWN: A(17) > G#(16) > G(15) > F#(14) > F(13) > E(12) > Eb(11) > D(10) > C#(9) > C(8) > B(7) > Bb(6) > A(5)

PRACTICE RULE
Use eighth-note triplets: one three-note group per beat. Begin 12-18 BPM below your clean Level 22 tempo.
Master the A-natural-minor cell alone, then use 2-3 neighboring rungs and pause one beat after each 36-note cell.
During the pause, release pressure and move the complete frame one fret. Repeat with both pick starts.
Then remove the pause, connect the ladder, reverse at fret 17, and return to fret 5.
Add 2 BPM after three clean round trips. Reduce 4-6 BPM after two errors, broken triplet pulse, dragged shift
noise, or rising tension. Advance after the complete route is clean with both pick starts on two separate days.

FULL ROUND-TRIP CHALLENGE
1. Start with A natural minor at fret 5.
2. Play all 13 cells without restarting the alternate-picking cycle.
3. Reverse after the fret-17 A-natural-minor cell and return through rungs 12-01.
4. Name each key and say 1-2-b3-4-5-b6-b7 before its first low root.
5. Repeat the complete route with the opposite starting stroke.
One complete route contains 25 cells x 36 notes = 900 picked notes.

CLEAN-ROUND STANDARD
No missing or added triplet; no pause at a group boundary; no accidental straight-eighth feel; no wrong scale tone;
no open string; no scrape or tempo change at the one-fret relocation; no accent caused by an early or late crossing;
and equal volume on both repeated octave-root notes at the turn.

AFTER THE BASIC LADDER IS CLEAN
1. Group accents: accent the first note of each triplet, then remove every group accent.
2. Scale-degree accents: accent only degrees 1, b3, 5, and b7 wherever they occur.
3. Crossing accents: accent the first note after each string crossing, then remove the accents.
4. Straight subdivision: play the same 36 notes as straight sixteenths without changing the order.
5. Direction: begin selected sessions at fret 17 and descend first.
6. Random entry: choose any root fret from 5-17, name the key, play its cell, and continue either direction.
These are Level 23 variations, not separate full levels.

MASTERY GATE BEFORE LEVEL 24
Complete the full fret-5 to fret-17 to fret-5 route with both pick starts on two separate days. Maintain a true
triplet pulse, preserve the 3-3-2 natural-minor geometry, keep all early and late crossings even, name every key,
and recover the correct triplet and key immediately after a deliberate random stop.
