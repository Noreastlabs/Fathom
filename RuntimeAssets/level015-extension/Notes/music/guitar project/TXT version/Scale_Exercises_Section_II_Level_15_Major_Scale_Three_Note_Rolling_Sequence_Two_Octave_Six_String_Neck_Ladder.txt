SCALE EXERCISES - SECTION II, LEVEL 15
MAJOR SCALE THREE-NOTE ROLLING SEQUENCE - TWO-OCTAVE SIX-STRING NECK LADDER: A TO A

PURPOSE
Extend the Level 14 three-note rolling sequence across the complete Level 13 two-octave, six-string
major-scale frame. Complete the full 78-note up/down rolling cell, release pressure, move the entire
hand-and-thumb frame exactly one fret, and repeat through every major key. Begin with A major at
low-E fret 5, climb to A major at fret 17, then reverse and return one fret at a time.

WHAT CHANGES FROM LEVEL 14
Level 14 introduced the overlap pattern inside one octave. Level 15 keeps the exact three-notes-forward,
one-degree-back logic but expands it from 8 scale positions to 15. The sequence now crosses every string,
passes through the middle-octave root, handles the B-string tuning offset, and reaches the high root before
reversing. The extra range is the new difficulty; the scale shape and sequence rule are unchanged.

IMPORTANT INTERPRETATION
Moving one fixed scale shape by one fret transposes it. A major is the starting rung, not the key of every rung.
A fixed-key A-major exercise in every neck position requires changing the note layout and remains a separate branch.

MAJOR-SCALE FORMULA
Scale positions: 1 through 15, covering two octaves.
Intervals per octave: whole - whole - half - whole - whole - whole - half
Positions 8 and 15 are octave roots; positions 9-14 repeat degrees 2-7 in the upper octave.

TWO-OCTAVE SIX-STRING SHAPE - ROOT FRET R ON LOW E
Low E string: R, R+2               Fingers 2,4
A string:     R-1, R, R+2          Fingers 1,2,4
D string:     R-1, R+1, R+2        Fingers 1,3,4
G string:     R-1, R+1, R+2        Fingers 1,3,4
B string:     R, R+2               Fingers 2,4
High e string: R-1, R              Fingers 1,2
The four-fret hand frame runs from R-1 through R+2.

ROLLING THREE-NOTE SEQUENCE
UP:   1-2-3 | 2-3-4 | 3-4-5 | 4-5-6 | 5-6-7 | 6-7-8 | 7-8-9 | 8-9-10 | 9-10-11 | 10-11-12 | 11-12-13 | 12-13-14 | 13-14-15
DOWN: 15-14-13 | 14-13-12 | 13-12-11 | 12-11-10 | 11-10-9 | 10-9-8 | 9-8-7 | 8-7-6 | 7-6-5 | 6-5-4 | 5-4-3 | 4-3-2 | 3-2-1
Each new triplet starts one scale position farther along. The line moves forward three notes, then rolls
back one position to begin the next group. Do not pause or reset at a triplet boundary, string crossing,
the middle-octave root, the high-root turn, or the one-fret relocation.

CELL AND PICKING RULE
The complete cell contains 26 triplets and 78 notes: 13 ascending groups and 13 descending groups.
The last note of 13-14-15 is followed immediately by the first note of 15-14-13, so the high root is
played twice at the turn. Because 78 is even, the next key begins with the same pick orientation.
D-start groups: D-U-D | U-D-U | D-U-D | U-D-U ... through all 26 groups.
U-start groups: U-D-U | D-U-D | U-D-U | D-U-D ... through all 26 groups.
Never restart the pick cycle at a new group, string crossing, octave, scale turn, or one-fret shift.

STRING-DISTRIBUTION AND CROSSING RULE
The underlying straight scale uses 2-3-3-3-2-2 notes per string ascending and 2-2-3-3-3-2 descending.
The rolling sequence repeatedly crosses a string and then revisits the previous scale position at the next
group boundary. Keep the rollback rhythmically invisible. Rebuild the assigned B/high-e fingering rather
than copying the G-string geometry across the B-string tuning offset.

NECK ROUTE
UP:   A(5) > Bb(6) > B(7) > C(8) > Db(9) > D(10) > Eb(11) > E(12) > F(13) > Gb(14) > G(15) > Ab(16) > A(17)
DOWN: A(17) > Ab(16) > G(15) > Gb(14) > F(13) > E(12) > Eb(11) > D(10) > Db(9) > C(8) > B(7) > Bb(6) > A(5)

PRACTICE RULE
Use eighth-note triplets: one three-note group per beat. Begin 15-20 BPM below your clean Level 14 tempo.
First master the 13 ascending triplets in A major, then the 13 descending triplets. Link them without
restarting the pick cycle and make the two high-root notes equal in length and volume. Next use 2-3
neighboring rungs and pause one beat after each 78-note cell while moving the complete frame one fret.
Repeat with both pick starts. Remove the pause, connect the ladder, reverse after the fret-17 cell, and
return to fret 5. Add 2 BPM after three clean round trips. Reduce 4-6 BPM after two errors, a broken triplet
pulse, a missed B-string offset, dragged shift noise, or rising tension. Advance after the complete route
is clean with both pick starts on two separate days.

FULL ROUND-TRIP CHALLENGE
Start at A major, low-E fret 5. Play rungs 01-13 without restarting the pick cycle. Reverse after the
fret-17 A-major cell and return through rungs 12-01. Say each key name aloud before its first low root.
Repeat the complete route with the opposite starting stroke.

CLEAN-ROUND STANDARD
No missing, added, or reordered triplet; no pause at a group boundary; no accidental straight-eighth feel;
no wrong scale tone or open string; no copied G-string geometry at the B-string offset; no scrape or tempo
change at the one-fret relocation; no accent caused by a string crossing; and equal volume on both high roots.

AFTER THE BASIC LADDER IS CLEAN
1. Group accents: accent the first note of each triplet, then remove every group accent.
2. Off-center accents: accent the second note of each triplet without changing the pulse.
3. Root accents: accent only positions 1, 8, and 15 wherever they occur inside the rolling line.
4. Sextuplets: play two triplet groups per beat at half the metronome marking.
5. Direction: begin selected sessions at fret 17 and descend first.
6. Random entry: choose any root fret from 5-17, name the key, play its cell, and continue either way.
These are Level 15 variations, not separate full levels.

MASTERY GATE BEFORE LEVEL 16
Complete the full A-to-A-to-A route with continuous triplet pulse, correct six-string geometry, silent
one-fret shifts, and both pick starts on two separate days. The next level changes the sequence length
from three-note groups to four-note groups, so do not advance while the triplet rollback still feels counted.
